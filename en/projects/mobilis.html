R&D project Sept. 2025 – Apr. 2026

Mobilis: a wrist rehabilitation device

R&D project · MSc year 1, Aix-Marseille University

Design of a modular orthosis for wrist rehabilitation after fracture.

10volunteers in the study
Class IMDR regulatory positioning
PEPITEselected to be put forward to the programme

Clinical context

Wrist fractures account for about 17.5% of adult fractures. After immobilisation, wrist mobility drops sharply: flexion falls from about 66° to 24° and extension from about 68° to 25°.

17.5%of adult fractures involve the wrist (Candela et al., 2022)
66° → 24°of flexion, before and after immobilisation (Dillingham et al., 2011)
68° → 25°of extension, before and after immobilisation

The problem. The literature shows that early active and passive mobilisation reduces pain and improves recovery, and that a device's usability drives patient perseverance. Yet orthoses on the market mostly assist grip rather than the rehabilitation of the wrist itself, and home exercises remain generic.

Our answer. A single modular orthosis that assists or resists flexion-extension depending on its setting, to support the patient throughout rehabilitation.

The device

A forearm cuff carries a wheel that sets the tension of elastic bands linked to a handle held in the hand. Depending on how it is fitted, the bands help the wrist move (assistance) or oppose it (resistance).

  • 3D-printed parts: PLA for rigid parts, flexible TPU for the handle.
  • Straps in polyester and nylon, ladder lock buckle, leather cases.
  • Elastic bands in natural and synthetic rubber.
  • Regulatory: Class I medical device (MDR 2017/745); ISO 13485, ISO 14971, ISO 12100 and ISO 10993 as references.

Sizing the elastic bands

Tension is derived from physiology: we take 20% of the maximal wrist flexion-extension moment, a load suited to early rehabilitation, convert it into a force at the hand, then into band elongation.

8 to 14 N·mmaximal flexion-extension moment in men, 5 to 9 N·m in women (Napper et al., 2023)
20%of that maximum, the load used for early rehabilitation
115 mmmean wrist-to-hand lever arm in men, 107 mm in women (Vergara et al., 2017)
F = M / Lfrom moment to force at the hand
F = k × Δlband stiffness
Δl = F / kelongation set with the wheel

With these values, the bands must provide a force of roughly 9 to 24 N depending on the patient.

Numerical simulation

The printed parts were checked by finite element analysis in SolidWorks: displacement of the PLA cuff, stress in the TPU handle, then fatigue life of the TPU.

The handle's fatigue strength is estimated from the S-N curve of printed TPU 95A, modelled with a Basquin law (σa = C × Nfb, with C ≈ 42.3 and b ≈ −0.088): the minimum computed life is about 8.7 million cycles.

Experimental study

Research question: to what extent can a modular assistance / resistance device improve wrist rehabilitation? The main objective was to measure its influence on range of motion and angular speed in flexion and extension; the secondary objective was to assess the user experience.

  • Participants: 10 healthy volunteers with no history of wrist pathology.
  • Preparation: fitting the device, a warm-up of 30 flexion-extensions, then baseline measurements.
  • Protocol: 10 flexions, 5 minutes of rest, 10 extensions, without the device and then with it in assistance and resistance modes, in random order.
  • Measurements: one camera and three reflective markers for maximal range of motion and angular speed; F-SUS usability questionnaire (10 to 15 minutes).
  • Statistics: no device vs assistance and no device vs resistance, using repeated-measures ANOVA or the Friedman test depending on the data, with post-hoc tests.

Results

In healthy subjects, range of motion does not change significantly: participants were not restricted to begin with. However, resistance mode clearly slows extension, showing that the device does engage the neuromuscular system. Participants found it very easy to use.

Limitations and outlook

Limitations. The sample is small and homogeneous (8 young healthy subjects, no patients in rehabilitation), which limits generalisation to the target population. Only one tension level was tested (20% of the maximal moment).

Outlook. Measure muscle activation by electromyography on the wrist flexors and extensors, explore several load levels (dose-response relationship), optimise the device, then validate it clinically with post-fracture patients.

The project ran from September to March, from the state of the art to the defence before a jury, and was selected to be put forward to the PEPITE programme (Pépite Provence).
