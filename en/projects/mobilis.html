R&D project Sept. 2025 – Apr. 2026

Mobilis: a wrist rehabilitation device

R&D project · MSc year 1, Aix-Marseille University

Design of an instrumented, modular orthosis for wrist rehabilitation after fracture: graded elastic assistance or resistance to movement, for progressive and personalised rehabilitation.

10volunteers in the study
Class IMDR regulatory positioning
PEPITEselected to be put forward to the programme

The need

Wrist fractures are among the most common fractures. After immobilisation, range of motion drops sharply, and rehabilitation relies on generic exercises that are often poorly followed at home. Existing orthoses mostly assist grip rather than rehabilitation.

Our question: to what extent can a modular assistance / resistance device improve wrist rehabilitation?

Design

  • Principle: an elastic band, tensioned with a wheel, assists or resists flexion-extension. Sizing starts from the maximal wrist moment (8-14 N·m in men, 5-9 N·m in women), scaled to 20% for early rehabilitation.
  • Materials: PLA and TPU printed parts, polyester and nylon straps, rubber elastic; chosen for strength, comfort and patient safety.
  • Numerical simulation: finite element analysis of the PLA and TPU parts, and fatigue analysis of 3D-printed TPU.
  • Regulatory: Class I medical device (MDR 2017/745), with ISO 13485, ISO 14971, ISO 12100 and ISO 10993 as references.

Evaluation

  • Protocol: 10 healthy volunteers, randomised no-device / assistance / resistance conditions, sets of flexion-extension.
  • Instrumentation: motion capture (camera and reflective markers) for range of motion and angular speed; surface EMG (Biopac MP150) on the flexor and extensor carpi radialis, 20-400 Hz Butterworth filtering and normalisation to % of maximal voluntary contraction.
  • Statistics: repeated-measures ANOVA or Friedman test depending on the data, with post-hoc tests.
  • Usability: F-SUS questionnaire, the validated French version of the System Usability Scale.

Results and next steps

No significant difference in range of motion in healthy subjects, consistent with the absence of pathology. However, angular speed decreased significantly in resistance mode: the device does engage the neuromuscular system. Usability scores showed good user acceptance.

Next steps: clinical validation with post-fracture patients, device optimisation and a dose-response study. The faculty spotted the project, which was selected to be put forward to the PEPITE programme (Pépite Provence), then defended before a jury.
